MseeZee

For community partners

Your community, raising together — online

MseeZee is a simple way for a neighbourhood to contribute towards a funeral, a family in need, or a local project. It works with organisations like yours that already carry this responsibility.

A conversation piece — MseeZee is in development and we are building it with a handful of partners first.

What your organisation already does

When someone in the community passes, families come to you. You help them arrange things, and often you help gather money — an envelope at the service, a WhatsApp group, transfers into your account. You count it, you bank it, you make sure it reaches the family.

MseeZee does not change that role. It gives you a page where your community can contribute from their phones, see the total climb, and leave a message — while the money still comes to your organisation, and you still decide how it reaches the family.

How the money reaches a family

  1. A family comes to you. You create a circle in a few minutes — their name, the story, the goal.
  2. The community contributes. Relatives in other provinces, colleagues, church members, old friends — they give from their phones by PayShap, bank transfer or card. No app to install.
  3. Contributions come to your organisation's account. Every rand is recorded against the circle, with the giver's name unless they chose to stay private.
  4. You disburse to the family the way you always have — paying the funeral home directly, buying groceries, handing over cash — now with a clean record of what came in and what went out.

Why a partner uses MseeZee

Reach further

People who can't be at the collection — family far away, colleagues, former neighbours — can still contribute.

No cash to handle

No envelope, no counting, no trip to the bank. It arrives in your account.

A clean record

Who gave, how much, and what it was spent on — protects your organisation and builds members' trust.

Families start immediately

Because a family is known to you, they don't wait on paperwork to begin collecting.

Your name vouches for it

The page carries your organisation's name. Givers trust a cause your organisation stands behind.

Free for families

MseeZee charges the grieving family nothing. The platform is kept running by small voluntary tips from contributors.

What MseeZee is not

  • Not a loan or credit — there is nothing to repay.
  • Not funeral insurance or a policy.
  • Not a replacement for your organisation — you stay in charge of the family relationship and the money.
  • Not a way for strangers to ask your community for money — only circles your organisation approves appear under your name.

What we would need from you

A one-time setup: your organisation's registration details and bank account, and the name of the person who authorises payments.

Then, per family: a few minutes to create and approve their circle.

From us: we set everything up with you, train whoever needs it, and stay reachable.

Founder's notes — not part of the leave-behind

Questions to ask in the first conversation

Listen more than you pitch. The goal is to understand how money already moves through this organisation and where it hurts.

How they work today

  1. When a family in your community loses someone, walk me through what happens — who do they come to, and what do you help with?
  2. How does a collection happen now — an envelope at the service, a WhatsApp group, transfers to an account?
  3. Roughly how many funerals does your organisation support in a month? Over a year?
  4. What does a typical collection raise, and what's the range from smallest to largest?
  5. Who handles the money — the counting, the banking, the record-keeping? How long does it take them?
  6. Do you already have an organisation bank account? Who is allowed to authorise a payment from it?

Where it hurts

  1. What is the hardest part of helping a family financially right now?
  2. Do relatives who live far away ask how they can contribute? What do you tell them?
  3. Has a collection ever fallen short of what the family needed? What happened then?
  4. Have you had disagreements about money — how much was raised, where it went? How did you settle them?
  5. How do you show a family, or your own members, where the money went?

Fit

  1. If your community could contribute to a funeral from their phones and the money came to your account, would that help or would it complicate things?
  2. Would families trust a contribution page with your organisation's name on it?
  3. What would make you decide not to use something like this?
  4. If this existed today, would you try it with the next funeral?
  5. Who else in the community — other societies, churches, the parlour you work with — should I be speaking to?

Practical

  1. What is your organisation's registration status — registered NPO, church, company, or an informal society?
  2. Would you be willing to be one of a small number of partners we build this with over the next few months, and give us honest feedback?
