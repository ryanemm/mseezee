How MseeZee moves money to beneficiaries

A payment provider has told us the model we first designed — routing contributions to beneficiaries and taking a fee, without a licence — is not supported. Three lawful structures remain. This brief lays out each so the two of you can choose a direction, then brief an attorney.

Prepared for MseeZee founders Date 9 Sep 2026 · updated 18 Sep 2026 Status Decided — Option B, partly confirmed Blocks Paystack written confirmation on individual beneficiaries

Update · 18 Sep 2026

Decided: MseeZee registers as a Pty Ltd now — for-profit in structure, with conversion to an NPC planned for some point after the pilot, not before. This is Option B below, with MseeZee's admin fee taken as a split, separate from what the beneficiary receives.

Confirmed workable, no further question needed: partner organisations (churches, burial societies, schools, registered NPOs) as the direct payout recipient — vetted once, onboarded as their own merchant. This is exactly how Option B was scoped.

Not yet confirmed — do not build on this until it is: the founders also want individual beneficiaries (a family, directly) added on an ongoing basis as their own direct payout recipient. This is the same shape Stitch already declined in writing. Paystack has not been asked this specific question yet — the open support thread has been about live-account activation, not this. A written answer from Paystack, using the same explicit framing that got a clear answer from Stitch, is needed before the individual-beneficiary path is treated as real. See §08 and §09 below, which still apply.

Section 01

Why this decision, now

Everything expensive downstream — which payment provider, how the company is registered, what the fee is, whether an individual family can start its own circle — depends on this one choice.

The original design was direct settlement: contributions would flow straight to the beneficiary through a payment provider, MseeZee would never hold the money, and we would take a small fee. Stitch's compliance team said this is not supported for an unlicensed intermediary. That is not a Stitch quirk — a platform that decides who gets paid, onboards a stream of new beneficiaries, and takes a fee from third-party funds is performing third-party payment services in substance, whether or not the money lands in our account.

The direction of regulation is the same way. South Africa's 2026 National Payment System reforms move to activity-based regulation with direct Reserve Bank oversight — you are regulated by what you do, not what you call yourself. And the two established players in this space, BackaBuddy and GivenGain, are both non-profit-structured and both hold and aggregate funds, disbursing after vetting. The elegant never-touch-the-money model is not how anyone actually operates here.

So the question is no longer "how do we avoid holding funds." It is "which of three lawful structures do we build."

Section 02 · Option A

Non-profit, hold and disburse

The BackaBuddy model. MseeZee is a Non-Profit Company that receives contributions into a safeguarded account, vets each circle, and pays out to the beneficiary — or directly to their funeral parlour or church — on a schedule or on request once vetting clears.

Option A at a glance

Recommended
Legal wrapper
MseeZee NPC, optionally paired with a for-profit Pty Ltd that owns the technology and IP and provides services to the NPC at arm's length.
Funds
Held in a ring-fenced pooled account under a sponsoring bank or PSP arrangement. Never mixed with operating money.
Individuals
Yes — families can start their own circles. BackaBuddy vets individual campaigns; so would we.
Cards
Fine. The gap between collection and the monthly payout absorbs chargebacks.
Revenue
0% mandatory fee on funeral circles; voluntary contributor tips; interest earned on funds held before payout; corporate social investment sponsorship; grants; B2B / white-label (billed through the Pty Ltd). Optionally a modest mandatory fee on wedding and community circles.
Founder economics
Market-related salaries from the NPC. Equity value accrues in the Pty Ltd services/IP company — must be genuinely arm's-length and disclosed.
Needs
NPC registration; a sponsoring bank relationship; a FICA / AML programme; a vetting process and the staff to run it; attorney sign-off; optionally PBO and Section 18A status for the funeral-relief activities.
Time to launch
Months. The bank relationship is the long pole.
Regulatory load
Moderate — you hold public funds, but as an NPC crowdfunding service, which is the established, tolerated pattern.
Precedent
BackaBuddy (NPC, holds funds, moved from a 7%+VAT fee to 0% + tips + float interest); GivenGain (Swiss foundation, aggregates and grants onward).

Why holding funds helps rather than hurts: vetting becomes a payout gate, not a pre-collection scramble; the payout buffer covers chargebacks; and "goal reached, now what" becomes simple. The things the direct model struggled with, this model solves.

Section 03 · Option B

Software only — the partner is the merchant

MseeZee never touches money. Only registered organisations receive funds, each through their own payment-provider merchant account, KYC'd normally as a business. MseeZee builds the campaign page that points contributions at that account.

Option B at a glance

Fastest
Legal wrapper
MseeZee Pty Ltd. Straightforward for-profit.
Funds
Never in MseeZee's control. Settle directly to the partner organisation's own account.
Individuals
As scoped here, no — a family raises through a partner organisation that hosts its circle and is accountable for the money. The founders want individuals to be able to receive directly too; that widens this option beyond what's reasoned through below and is pending written confirmation from Paystack — see the update note at the top of this document.
Cards
The partner's choice, on their own account.
Revenue
SaaS subscription billed to partner organisations; optionally a separate, voluntary donor tip paid into MseeZee's own account as its own income — two separate transactions, never commingled; B2B / white-label.
Founder economics
Ordinary for-profit — equity, dividends and any sale value sit in the Pty Ltd.
Needs
A partner-onboarding process; integration with one or two providers' existing sub-merchant / split products; terms of use positioning MseeZee as software; an attorney's confirmation that this stays outside third-party-payment-provider designation.
Time to launch
Weeks. No fund handling, no bank relationship.
Regulatory load
Lowest — you are a software vendor, in the mould of Donorbox.
Precedent
Church-giving plugins and donation-software vendors that integrate with a charity's existing merchant account.

The trade-off: growth is entirely partner-led — no viral individual funerals — so it depends on how quickly you can sign burial societies, churches and parlours, and revenue is subscription rather than transaction volume.

Section 04 · Option C

Licensed payment facilitator under sponsorship

A formal payment-facilitator agreement in which a sponsoring bank or PSP is accountable for MseeZee's compliance. With that in place, close to the original direct-settlement design becomes possible.

Individuals

Licensed South African PSPs and payfacs.

When it is worth it: if being a payments or fintech company — not a fundraising organisation — is the vision, and you can fund the runway to get there.

Section 06

What each means for what's already built

The frontend prototype was built behind a data boundary specifically so it survives this decision. Concretely:

  • All three: the circle pages, contribution flow, area discovery and create wizard are unaffected. Only the payment step changes.
  • The contribution screen already carries a 0% + optional tip framing — that fits Options A and B directly.
  • Options A and C add a payout / disbursement view for organisers and partners later. Option B does not — funds settle to the partner and MseeZee never shows a balance.
  • Option B also narrows the create flow: "start your own circle" becomes "start a circle with a partner organisation."

Section 07

Recommendation

Lead with Option A

It is the proven path in this exact market, it keeps the whole product — including a family starting its own funeral circle — and the hold-and-vet flow makes fraud control stronger, not weaker. Founder upside is preserved through the Pty Ltd services company. Start NPC registration and open conversations with a sponsoring bank now.

Choose Option B instead if staying a straightforward for-profit and launching in weeks matters more than individual families being able to raise directly, and you are confident you can sign partner organisations quickly. It is also a clean first step — you can launch on B and move to A later.

Choose Option C only if the ambition is explicitly a payments company at scale, and you can fund 6–12 months of compliance work before revenue.

The deciding factor is not really regulatory — it is what kind of business the two of you want to run.

Section 08

Questions for the two of you

Answer these together before the attorney meeting — they determine which option is even on the table.

  • Is the goal a sustainable mission organisation (good salaries, a services company with value) or a venture-scale equity outcome? A points at the first; B and C keep the second open.
  • Can you fund 6–12 months of compliance runway with no revenue? If not, C is out.
  • Is "an individual family can start its own circle" essential to the vision, or is partner-hosted acceptable? If essential, B is out.
  • Do you have — or can you realistically build — a relationship with a sponsoring bank? Needed for A and C.
  • Are you willing to run vetting operations — staff reviewing circles before payout? Required for A.
  • How fast can you sign 10 partner organisations (burial societies, churches, parlours)? This is the growth ceiling for B and the fast-path for A.

Section 09

Brief for the attorney

Engage a South African fintech / payments attorney — Michalsons, ENSafrica, or Werksmans all have the practice. Bring the Stitch email as evidence of the constraint. Ask, for whichever option you have chosen:

If Option A

  • An NPC crowdfunding platform holding funds in a safeguarded account, disbursing after vetting, under bank sponsorship — what exactly is required? Trust-account structure? Audit obligations?
  • Where do FICA obligations sit — with us, the sponsoring bank, or the payout recipient?
  • Is the funeral-relief activity eligible for PBO status and Section 18A donor deductions? What about the wedding / personal circles?
  • Is the NPC + arm's-length Pty Ltd services structure sound, and how must it be documented?

If Option B

  • Does "the partner organisation is merchant of record, MseeZee bills SaaS and separately takes voluntary donor tips" stay outside third-party-payment-provider designation?
  • What must the terms of use and the partner agreement say to hold that position?

If Option C

  • The realistic path to payfac sponsorship or a PSP licence under the 2026 NPS regime — timeline, capital, and which sponsoring institutions are taking on partners.

All options

  • POPIA: register an Information Officer with the Information Regulator; confirm our consent, retention and breach-notification obligations.
  • Consumer-protection disclosure on the "contributions are final" position.
