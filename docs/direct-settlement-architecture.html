Section 01

The model in one picture

A contribution travels from the giver to the family through a licensed payment provider. Sisonke builds the circle page, the sharing loop, the verification, and the dashboard — but the money never lands in an account Sisonke owns.

Three properties define the model and everything downstream depends on them:

  • The beneficiary is the merchant of record. The family — or a verified organisation acting for them — holds the relationship with the PSP and is KYC'd by the PSP, not by Sisonke.
  • Each circle gets its own virtual account issued by the PSP in the beneficiary's name. Contributions land there and sweep to the beneficiary's real bank account on a schedule or on demand.
  • Sisonke's accounts only ever receive Sisonke's own money — the optional contributor tip and, later, subscription income. Never the principal, not even in transit.

Section 02

Regulatory positioning

South Africa's payment rules turn on a single question: does money ever vest in an account the platform owns or controls? If it does — even for a moment — Sisonke becomes a Third-Party Payment Provider under the South African Reserve Bank's framework (the National Payment System Act and Directive 1 of 2007), which requires PSP sponsorship, a ring-fenced trust account, independent audits, and registration with the payments industry body.

Direct settlement avoids that entirely. The PSP receives and holds the funds under its own licence — which it is permitted to do — and Sisonke stays a technology and trust-services provider.

The bright line

Money must never enter an account Sisonke owns or controls. In practice that means:

  • The beneficiary or a verified proxy is the merchant of record with the PSP; the virtual account holding a circle's funds is theirs, under the PSP's licence.
  • There is no "sweep to Sisonke, then pay out" step — ever, including for refunds, disputes, or corrections.
  • There is no manual "we'll collect it and forward it" fallback for awkward cases. If a case can't be served without Sisonke holding funds, it is out of scope until the managed-payout product exists.
  • Sisonke's bank accounts receive only platform fees and tips, which are ordinary income, not third-party money.

Attorney sign-off checklist — before any public collection

A SA fintech attorney (Michalsons, ENSafrica, or Werksmans) must confirm, in writing:

  • the PSP contract structure and virtual-account ownership place funds outside Sisonke's control;
  • the fee-split mechanism is characterised as Sisonke's service income, not held funds;
  • the terms of use position Sisonke as a conduit that does not guarantee how funds are used;
  • FICA obligations sit with the PSP and Smile ID, not Sisonke;
  • consumer-protection and POPIA disclosures are adequate;
  • an Information Officer is registered with the Information Regulator.

Section 04

Choosing the PSP

The PSP relationship is the long pole of the whole project — onboarding and contract terms for a marketplace-style model take weeks. Start conversations with two providers in parallel immediately.

Requirements

  • Connected-account / sub-merchant model with KYC performed by the PSP.
  • A per-circle virtual account or sub-account whose funds are owned by the beneficiary.
  • Automatic fee split routing Sisonke's cut to a Sisonke account at settlement.
  • PayShap and Instant EFT / pay-by-bank support.
  • Signed, idempotent webhooks for payment and payout events.
  • Payouts to SA bank accounts with FICA handled by the PSP.
  • A daily settlement report — file or API — for reconciliation.
  • A usable sandbox and a realistic onboarding timeline.
ProviderFit for this modelWatch-outs
Stitch primary Platform / marketplace native. Pay-by-bank, PayShap, virtual accounts, and payouts in one API. API-first with strong docs. Confirm virtual-account ownership sits with the beneficiary; confirm split-fee settlement; newer than the incumbents.
Peach Payments fallback Established SA aggregator. Split settlement, PayShap, PayByLink. Good route if cards are added later. Marketplace payout flows less turnkey than Stitch; more configuration.
Paystack contingency Excellent developer experience, subaccounts and splits, ZAR support. Existing internal prototype. Weaker on pay-by-bank / PayShap breadth and on true per-beneficiary virtual accounts in SA.

Recommendation

Stitch as primary. Open a Peach account in parallel as a hedge against onboarding delay and as the likely path if cards are introduced. Final choice is gated on written confirmation of virtual-account ownership and the fee-split mechanism.

Section 06

Tracking money you never hold

Sisonke never sees the bank account, so its ledger cannot be the source of truth for balances. The PSP is the source of truth; Sisonke keeps a mirror and reconciles against it every night. Three signals feed the mirror:

  1. Payment-intent creation — Sisonke initiates, so it knows what inflow to expect.
  2. Signed webhook events — payment.authorized, payment.settled, payout.completed — near-real-time truth, processed idempotently by PSP event id.
  3. The daily settlement file — authoritative end-of-day truth. Every line is matched to a ledger entry; any mismatch pages an operator.

Ledger rules

  • Append-only ledger_entries, double-entry, amounts in integer cents with an explicit currency column.
  • Entries are created only from confirmed webhook or settlement events — never from an optimistic client signal.
  • Every entry carries the PSP event id that produced it; processing is upsert-safe on that id.
  • The nightly reconciliation job pulls the settlement file, matches each line, produces an exceptions report, and pages on anything unmatched beyond a threshold or older than 24 hours.

Section 08

Beneficiary onboarding & KYC

Direct settlement moves the KYC burden to the front of the process: a circle cannot collect until the beneficiary can receive.

The flow

  1. Organiser creates a circle and names the beneficiary.
  2. Beneficiary (or proxy) verifies a phone number.
  3. Smile ID check — SA ID plus selfie liveness, validated against Home Affairs.
  4. Bank-account verification — account-holder name match, via the PSP or a bureau.
  5. PSP creates the connected account and issues the circle's virtual account.
  6. The circle moves to collecting.

The funeral-timeline problem

A funeral is often four days away and full KYC takes time. Three mitigations, in order of preference:

  • Pre-verified proxies. Churches, burial societies, and funeral parlours onboard once, then stand as beneficiary-of-record for many families and disburse onward. This is also the strongest distribution channel Sisonke has.
  • Fast-path KYC. Smile ID selfie-plus-ID clears in minutes when documents are clean; bank verification uses an instant name check. Target: under two hours, with a visible progress indicator.
  • Share-first, collect-later. The circle can be created and shared immediately in a pending-verification state; the "Contribute" button activates only once the virtual account exists.

Decision needed

Allow contributions to be pledged before KYC clears, or block collection entirely until it does? Recommendation: block collection, with the proxy fast-path as the pressure valve. Pledges that can't be honoured are worse for trust than a short, visible wait.

Section 10

Contribution flow

  1. Contributor opens the circle page — server-rendered, with an OpenGraph image showing live progress. No account required.
  2. They pick an amount and a rail, enter a display name and optional message, and set the anonymity toggle.
  3. The web app calls the payments service, which creates a payment intent, writes a contribution row as initiated, and returns a PSP-hosted payment URL or PayShap request.
  4. The contributor completes payment at the PSP or in their banking app, then returns to a thank-you page — status still provisional.
  5. The PSP webhook arrives; the payments service verifies the signature, processes it idempotently, and on payment.settled writes the ledger entry, marks the contribution settled, recomputes the circle total, and enqueues notifications.
  6. Notifications go out: SMS and WhatsApp to the organiser ("R200 from Thandi M."), a receipt to the contributor if contact was given, and a thank-you prompt to the organiser.
  7. Funds settle to the circle virtual account; the PSP sweeps them to the beneficiary's bank account on schedule or on demand.

If payment fails or is abandoned, the contribution is marked failed or abandoned, no ledger entry is written, and the contributor gets a retry link. A lost webhook is caught by the nightly settlement reconciliation.

Section 11

Fees without touching the principal

  • The PSP performs a split at settlement: the beneficiary's virtual account receives the net, and a Sisonke account receives the platform fee (if any) plus the contributor tip.
  • Recommended: 0% platform fee on funeral circles. A mandatory cut from a grieving family is the wrong posture. Contributors can add an optional tip to Sisonke at checkout — suggested by default, removed in one tap.
  • The PSP transaction fee is either shown transparently as deducted from the beneficiary's amount, or covered by a "cover the fee" toggle for the contributor (recommended default: on).
  • Later monetisation: premium accounts for proxies and organisations (multi-circle dashboards, branding, reporting), and B2B relationships with employers and insurers — never a cut from families.
  • Every fee movement is ordinary income into Sisonke's own account. No third-party funds are involved, so this does not compromise the regulatory posture.

Section 12

Fraud & trust

Funeral-fundraising fraud is the platform's biggest reputational risk, and direct settlement removes the holdback lever — so defence moves entirely to before collection starts.

  • Verification gates collection. A circle above the unverified cap cannot take money until identity, and for larger amounts evidence, is confirmed.
  • Duplicate detection. The same deceased name, area, and date across circles is flagged for review.
  • Velocity and anomaly checks. Sudden spikes, many small contributions from one instrument, and organiser–beneficiary identity overlap raise flags.
  • Community reporting on every circle, with a staff takedown SLA and the ability to freeze a virtual account through the PSP.
  • Reputation. Organiser and beneficiary identity is bound to a verified ID and carries across circles.
  • Transparency as deterrent. Public progress, supporter counts, and required updates make fabricated circles harder to sustain.
  • Audit everything. Append-only log of every money action and every staff action, with reason codes.

Section 15

Non-functional requirements

  • POPIA. Register an Information Officer with the Information Regulator. Lawful basis and consent captured; data-subject access and erasure supported; 72-hour breach notification. Retention: KYC documents held per FICA (five years), contribution records five years, then contributor contact purged.
  • Data residency. Postgres, object storage, and KYC documents in South Africa. Vercel's edge caches only public, non-PII content.
  • Encryption. TLS everywhere; PII encrypted at rest; SA ID numbers and bank details field-level encrypted with a managed key. KYC documents in a separate, access-audited bucket with signed URLs under five minutes.
  • PCI. Scope stays SAQ-A — no card data at launch. If cards are added, PSP-hosted fields only.
  • Load shedding. Every money flow is idempotent and resumable; form state is saved client-side; payment status is always re-fetchable; nothing depends on a single long-lived connection.
  • Performance. The contribution path ships under 100 KB of JavaScript and works on 3G; time-to-interactive under three seconds on a low-end Android. Public pages are server-rendered and cached.
  • Accessibility & language. WCAG AA. Internationalisation scaffold from day one: launch in English, then isiZulu, isiXhosa, Afrikaans, and Sesotho.
  • Observability. Sentry for errors, PostHog for product analytics with PII masked, uptime checks on the create-circle, contribute, and webhook endpoints, alerts on reconciliation exceptions and webhook backlog.
